Why we don't build our own — and what we choose instead.

A 15-minute walkthrough before the live demo

01 / Hook

So — what would it take to build your own
payment system from scratch?

Think for 90 seconds

Don't write code. List the moving parts. What does the user click, what does your server do, and — crucially — what happens to the money between their bank and yours?

02 / The hidden iceberg

You'd need to build, then operate, all of this.

Each box is not a feature.
Each box is a team.

And every connection between boxes is a contract, a reconciliation report, and a 3 a.m. incident waiting to happen.

03 / The legal floor

Before you write a line of code, regulators set the floor.

  • 01
    PCI DSS
    Anyone who touches card data. Level 1 audits are annual and expensive.
  • 02
    PSD2 & SCA
    EU Strong Customer Authentication — two-factor on most card payments.
  • 03
    GDPR
    Personal data: lawful basis, retention, breach notification within 72 hours.
  • 04
    AML / KYC
    Know Your Customer. Sanctions screening. Suspicious transaction reporting.
  • 05
    Payment Institution licence
    In Finland: Finanssivalvonta (FIN-FSA). Capital requirements, governance, fit-and-proper directors.
Worth knowing

A breach of card data isn't a bug. It's a regulatory event — and the fines have eight digits.

PCI DSS, PSD2, GDPR, AML and licence rules are not optional. They apply whether you're a startup of two or a bank of two thousand.

04 / Time & liability

The real reason we integrate isn't code. It's risk transfer.

Build it yourself

12–24 months to MVP

  • • Annual PCI audits
  • • Bank partnerships to negotiate
  • • 24/7 ops team
  • • You carry the breach liability
  • • You explain it to the regulator
Integrate a provider

Days to weeks

  • • Provider handles PCI scope
  • • Bank relationships pre-built
  • • Their on-call, not yours
  • • Breach liability shared / reduced
  • • You pay ~1.4–2.9% per transaction

Thought: if you save 2% by building your own — but spend a year building it — at what monthly revenue does that ever pay back?

05 / The landscape

The market is not Stripe alone.

Provider Sweet spot What it gets you
Stripe Developer-led startups, global SaaS Best docs in the industry, hosted Checkout, 40+ countries
Adyen Enterprise, marketplaces One platform across cards, wallets, in-store. Better pricing at scale.
PayPal / Braintree Consumer trust, B2C Brand recognition. Wallet-style "Pay with PayPal" button buyers know.
Klarna EU Nordic retail, BNPL Buy-now-pay-later, invoice-after-delivery. Huge in Nordic e-commerce.
Paytrail FI Finnish merchants Native bank-button payments (Nordea, OP, S-Pankki) without each integration.
Mollie EU EU SMBs Simpler than Stripe, strong iDEAL / Bancontact / SEPA coverage.

Card pricing is roughly comparable (~1.4% + €0.25 in EU, ~2.9% + $0.30 in US). The differences are payment methods, geography, and developer experience.

06 / Why Stripe (for this course)

Five reasons — and one caveat.

  • 01
    Documentation
    Still the gold standard. Examples in five languages, kept current.
  • 02
    Developer experience
    APIs you can read and reason about. Idempotency keys. Webhooks that are debuggable.
  • 03
    Hosted Checkout
    Drops you out of PCI scope almost entirely — you never see a card number.
  • 04
    Global with one SDK
    Same code path serves cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, SEPA, Klarna, and more.
  • 05
    Transparent pricing
    Published on the website. No enterprise sales call to find out what it costs.
Caveat

Stripe is the right default — not the right answer.

At scale, Adyen often wins on price. For a Finnish-only customer base, Paytrail's bank buttons convert better. For consumer-facing B2C, PayPal's brand can matter more than your API.

The skill we're teaching is how to evaluate, not which logo to use.

07 / The flow we'll build

One redirect. One webhook. That's the integration.

The teaching beat: never trust the redirect. The user could close their tab, lose connection, or type the URL manually. The webhook is Stripe telling your server, server-to-server, that the money actually moved.

08 / Mobile

"Mobile payments" is actually three different things.

Most developers — and most product managers — confuse these. The distinctions are worth ~30% of your revenue.

Path A — Web in app
0%

Mobile browser or in-app webview redirects to Stripe Checkout. Same flow as desktop. No platform commission.

Path B — Apple Pay / Google Pay
0%

A wallet, not a billing system. The card behind it is still processed by Stripe. Apple Pay ≠ In-App Purchase.

Path C — In-App Purchase
30%

Digital goods consumed inside an app (subscriptions, coins, premium features). On iOS you must use Apple's billing.

The line: physical goods and real-world services (Uber, Amazon, Airbnb) can use Stripe inside an app at 0%. Digital goods consumed in-app cannot.

FLAG  09 / The Apple commission
30%

on every in-app dollar of digital goods.

If you ship digital, model this from day one.

  • 15%
    Small Business Program
    If you make < $1M/year through the App Store.
  • 15%
    Year-2+ subscriptions
    After 12 months of continuous subscriber retention.
  • 17 / 13%
    EU (DMA, 2024+)
    Alternative billing now allowed in the EU — at reduced but still meaningful fees.
  • 0%
    Physical goods & real-world services
    Uber, Amazon, Airbnb, Deliveroo — all use their own payment systems.

Pricing reality: a €9.99/month digital subscription nets you €6.99. A SaaS sold on the web instead nets ~€9.70. Same product, 38% revenue difference.

10 / Rules of thumb

When to reach for what.

If you're building… Default to Why
A web SaaS / subscription product Stripe Best subscription primitives, billing portal, tax automation.
An e-commerce store selling physical goods Stripe or Adyen Shopify-tier defaults; Adyen if you're enterprise.
A Finnish consumer product Paytrail + cards Finns expect to pay with their bank button. Conversion gap is real.
A retailer with BNPL demand Klarna alongside cards Cart-abandonment killer for Nordic / EU retail.
A mobile app selling digital goods Apple/Google IAP No choice. Price the 15–30% commission into your model.
A mobile app selling real-world services Stripe in-app 0% platform fee. Apple's rules permit this — don't accidentally use IAP.
11 / Discuss

Open questions, no single answer.

  1. If a customer disputes a charge as fraud, who eats the loss — you, your bank, or Stripe?
  2. A client says: "Let's save 2.9% by building our own payments." What's your reply, in one sentence?
  3. A Finnish startup sells to Germany, France and the US. What provider mix would you propose?
  4. You're building a mobile-first language-learning app. How does the 30% Apple commission shape your price?
  5. A user closes the tab before the success page renders. Are they charged? Does your server know?
